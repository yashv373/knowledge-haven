## About Me

Yashvardhan Singh is an undergraduate student pursuing a B.Tech. in EE - VLSI Design at the Manipal Institute of Technology (Class of 2027). 

His professional experience features a position as an Intern in the Frontend IP Platform Design Team at **STMicroelectronics**, which began in July 2026. Prior to this, he served as a Hardware Intern at Ecosight Technologies (BeePrecise), where he designed a custom 4G SIM PCB for their product and supported the procurement of in-house manufacturing machinery. In his initial days of undergrad, he also worked as an electronics engineering team member for the Manipal Hyperloop student team, LoopMIT. 

During his undergrad, Yashvardhan Co-Founded and was the former President of **ChipMIT**, established as India's first undergraduate chip design club. 

His technical competencies have been recognized through several instances, including being named a Samsung Fellowship Recipient under the ISWDP Cohort 5. He won the AI Accelerator RTL Challenge at the Silicon Sprint 2025 Hackathon by NITK, RVCE, and Tenstorrent, and secured the Best Hack at the M# Manipal Hackathon 2024. Additionally, he is a VLSID 2026 Local Fellowship Recipient and was awarded the MAHE Directorate of Research Incentive.

As of September 2026, his research portfolio includes five independent lifetime citations, three preprints, one journal paper, and one conference paper under review. As an Undergraduate Student Researcher at MIT Manipal, his work included analyzing CMOS-MTJ sense amplifiers for hybrid CIM systems, which resulted in a first-author publication for the *IEEE JxCDC* journal. He previously completed a Summer Research Internship at IIT BHU, where he focused on a hybrid MTJ SPICE model. His research work also includes the semicustom VLSI design and analysis of a 32-bit Brent-Kung adder, and a preprint on Junctionless Negative Capacitance FinFETs analysis.

His core research interests include hardware acceleration, digital ASIC design, open-source silicon design, machine learning for VLSI EDA, and hybrid post-CMOS technologies like CMOS-MTJ (spintronics).

This site serves as my personal, living notebook. It's a raw, unfiltered log of my journey through Digital Design, Computer Architecture, and Hardware Engineering. You'll find my study notes, RTL practice logs (such as my progression through the Harris & Harris DDCA textbook), and insights from building custom hardware accelerators.
